# Mode: envision — design the UI of tomorrow, starting from humans

Use for: "what will this product look like in 3/5/10 years?", AI-native redesigns, new paradigms (agents, ambient, voice, spatial, wearables), vision decks/films, north-star concepts, "how will AI change this workflow?".

**Stance:** technology changes fast and humans change slowly. Design the future from the human constants outward, not from the technology inward. A vision that ignores perception, memory, attention, motor limits, trust and the need for control is a demo, not a future.

Load: `references/future/futures-human-constants.md` and `references/future/futures-methods.md` (always). Add `futures-visions-that-came-true.md` for the long view (Bush, Licklider, Engelbart, Kay, Weiser, Victor), `ai-patterns-exemplars-failures-and-tomorrow.md` for AI paradigms, and `ai-ethics-gate.md` before delivering.

## Steps
1. **Anchor the human job.** What does the person actually want done, and how will they feel about it? Write the job independent of any interface ("know my money is OK", not "open the banking app"). This is the part that survives every paradigm shift.
2. **List the human constants that bind this job** (from the human-constants file): attention budget, ~4-chunk working memory, need for control and understanding, trust formation, motor/ergonomic limits, social context, fatigue, accessibility.
3. **Scan the signals:** what is technically emerging (models, agents, on-device AI, sensors, form factors), what is shifting socially (norms, regulation), and what already ships at the edges. Tag each one established / emerging / speculative, and cite sources.
4. **Use one futures method**, picked to fit the timebox: futures cone (probable / plausible / possible / preferable), Three Horizons, a 2×2 scenario grid on the two most uncertain drivers, or backcasting from a preferable 2030 to what ships next quarter.
5. **Generate 3 concept directions** at different distances: H1 (AI inside today's UI, shippable now), H2 (a hybrid where the UI and an agent share the work), H3 (a native new paradigm: intent-first, ambient, multimodal). For each, give the core interaction in one sentence, a key-moment storyboard, what the human controls, and what the system does on its own.
6. **Stress-test each one against people:**
   - Can the user predict, see, interrupt and undo what the system does?
   - Is trust calibrated? Can errors be seen and are they cheap?
   - Does it keep people skilled rather than deskilling them? Is it still accessible?
   - Does it respect attention? Does it pass the AI ethics gate?

   Also run one master lens: Weiser's calm technology, or Victor's humane representation.
7. **Backcast to now:** what is the smallest shippable step toward the preferable future, and which experiment would tell us we're right (→ `modes/experiment.md`)? Name the leading indicator to watch.
8. **Make it tangible:** a key-screen prototype (→ `modes/prototype.md` / `modes/figma.md`), a storyboard, or a short design-fiction narrative ("a day in 2030 with...").

## Output
Human job & constants → signals (tagged) → method used → 3 horizon concepts (storyboard + control model + risks) → human stress-test table → recommended direction → first shippable step + experiment → prototype or storyboard.

Be bold in H3 and rigorous in H1. Say plainly which parts are speculation.

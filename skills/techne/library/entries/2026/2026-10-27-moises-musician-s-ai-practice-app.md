## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Use AI to serve practice, not replace the player: split any song into stems so musicians 'mute the part you play, or solo the one you're learning' — and state plainly on the homepage that the product never trains on your data.

## Why it works
- Apple's 2024 App Store Awards named Moises iPad App of the Year, citing that it 'empowers musicians on iPad to hone their craft with powerful AI tools' [award jury — expert recognition, not performance data].
- The job is framed in a musician's verbs: practice, perform, create, collaborate; stem separation into '27 stem types' is explained by its use ('Upload a song and play along in seconds… Mute the part you play, or solo the one you're learning').
- Trust copy on the homepage FAQ: 'Moises will never train on your data' and models are trained 'only on licensed materials' — addresses the creative community's main AI fear head-on.
- Site caveat (observed 2026-10-27): generic performer-video hero and a first-layer cookie notice with only 'Manage Preferences', no reject button; app UI itself not inspected in this run.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: AI in creative and learning tools — position the model as an instrument that amplifies the user's skill; any AI product handling user content should state its training-data policy up front.
- Doesn't: products where the AI output is the end product rather than a practice aid.

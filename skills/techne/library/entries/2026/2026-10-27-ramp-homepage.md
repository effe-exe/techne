## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Show the product's real output as the hero and back it with running counters (receipts processed, fields coded) — operational proof beats illustrative metaphor for B2B.

## Why it works
- Monochrome white + near-black (lab 2.8) page with one loud accent: chartreuse/lime on the primary CTAs ("See a demo", "Get started for free"). A single saturated colour makes the conversion path impossible to miss.
- Hero headline 64px Lausanne at weight 400, line-height 1.0, tight; subline at ~20px muted; email-first form inside a hairline-bordered 12px-radius container.
- A tiny caps label with a monospace-style ticking counter ("US corporate payments processed by Ramp: 0.952…%") and a bottom ticker bar of live-looking stats ("Receipts processed 1,286,383 · Accounting fields coded 261,877…") turn the page into a status board.
- Hero visual is an actual product screen (transaction detail, approval recommendation, bills on mobile) in a faux-browser frame on a dotted-grid background. Motion 0.3s cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1); DNA found prefers-reduced-motion rules.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: B2B tools whose value is throughput/automation; landing pages that need one unmistakable CTA.
- Doesn't: counters only work if the numbers are real and sourced; fake tickers are a trust liability.

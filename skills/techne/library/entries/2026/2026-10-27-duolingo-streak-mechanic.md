## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Make the daily habit unit as small as possible (one lesson keeps the streak) and decouple it from ambitious goals — then measure retention, not just engagement.

## Why it works
- Duolingo's blog reports that separating the daily goal from the streak (one lesson = streak extended, regardless of XP goal) produced +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% daily active learners, and +10.5% share of daily learners on a streak in 20 days (+19% among new learners) [strong — company-published A/B, relative changes].
- Same post: an earlier change 'increased the number of learners on a 7+ day streak by over 40%'; it found almost 40% of learners active two days in a row *without* a streak had the 'intense' goal — over-ambitious goals broke habits.
- The live product reinforces the loop with a flame counter, streak freezes and celebration screens; App Store listing shows 5.5M ratings at 4.7, Editors' Choice, #1 in Education.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: any habit product (fitness, finance saving, journaling, language) — lower the daily bar, protect the streak with forgiveness (freezes), reward continuity.
- Doesn't: tasks where daily repetition isn't the goal; and streak loss-aversion can tip into guilt — pair with forgiveness and quiet modes (see Gentler Streak in the seed gallery).

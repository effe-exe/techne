## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Let readers switch the hype off: a 'Marketing Mode ON' toggle signals the brand knows its audience distrusts marketing — and the copy ('considered "not bad" by millions of developers') stays wry.

## Why it works
- Headline 'Code breaks, fix it faster' in Dammit Sans 88/700 with 'breaks,' rotated and set in hot pink — the word literally breaks the baseline.
- A fixed 'Marketing Mode ON' switch sits top-right; the sub-line is self-deprecating: 'Application monitoring software considered "not bad" by millions of developers.'
- Below: an illustrated purple robot surrounded by product-fragment cards (Errors, Replays, Logs, Traces, Seer root-cause list) in the brand's deep purple rgb(31,22,51) with lime and pink accents.
- Uppercase Rubik nav (14/500, +0.2px), Monaco for code (194 nodes); square-ish 8px CTAs: white 'GET STARTED' and pink-outlined 'GET DEMO'. Reduced-motion honored, WebGL present.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: developer audiences, any brand whose users are sceptical of marketing.
- Doesn't: regulated or formal contexts; irony ages fast if overused.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Exceptional brand craft does not neutralise incentive design: a lottery banner that rewards trading volume turns a beautiful page into a nudge toward risk.

## Why it works
- Craft (worth studying): h1 "Trade All in One Place" in Martina Plantijn serif at 110px/400, line-height 1.0, white on a dark full-bleed architectural video; Phonic sans for h2 (52px, -1.5px) and UI; Capsule Sans Text for buttons. One neon accent rgb(204,255,0) for CTA pills and an animated chart line.
- The lesson (why anti-example): a full-width lime strip directly under the nav reads "Sweepstakes: Trade eligible crypto for a chance to win a share of $700K in crypto prizes… Ends November 22, 2026. Free entry available. Terms apply." — a prize mechanic tied to trading volatile assets, in the most prominent slot on the page.
- Context: Robinhood previously removed its confetti animation after regulators (Massachusetts, 2020–21) criticised gamified trading. The pattern recurs in a new form. [news/regulatory — moderate]
- Also: DNA found no prefers-reduced-motion rules and no CSS transitions on an 89%-media page.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Steal: the serif-display + geometric-sans pairing and single-neon accent for a brand that wants to feel cultural rather than corporate.
- Avoid: rewards, streaks or lotteries attached to risky financial actions (trading, borrowing, betting); disclosure ("free entry available") does not fix the incentive.

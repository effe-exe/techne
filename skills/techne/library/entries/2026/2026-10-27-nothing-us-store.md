## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Derive the whole UI type system from one hardware signature (the dot-matrix display): dot font for product names and nav, a crisp serif for the promise, mono for small labels.

## Why it works
- Product name "HEADPHONE (1) PRO" and the "NOTHING (R)" wordmark are set in the Ndot dot-matrix face; the promise "Studio-level precision for our most advanced headphone ever." is NType82 at 48px (a sharp serif-like display) in white on black; small UI ("EXPLORE OFFERS · LEARN MORE", "DISCOVER >") in mono caps (Geist Mono / Lattera Mono).
- Navigation is a floating, centred, translucent grey bar (~576px wide, 8px radius) with only menu, wordmark and bag; a dismissible offers strip docks beneath it.
- Background is a sparse dot grid on near-black rgb(4,4,4), echoing the dot-matrix motif; the product render floats in it. One white square "DISCOVER" button.
- Region-mismatch modal (seen on the UK URL) uses the same language: dot-matrix pin icon, "LOOKS LIKE YOU'RE IN: UNITED STATES", primary "GO TO: UNITED STATES" and a text-link "STAY ON UNITED KINGDOM" — honest, reversible. Caveat: no reduced-motion rules detected.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: hardware or brands with a distinctive physical signature (displays, materials, patterns) to translate into type and texture.
- Doesn't: dot-matrix type is for short labels only; long copy needs the conventional faces (as Nothing does).

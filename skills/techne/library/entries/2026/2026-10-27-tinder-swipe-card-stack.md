## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Reduce a high-volume judgement to a single binary gesture on one card at a time — but only where quick, low-stakes decisions are actually healthy.

## Why it works
- The swipe (right = like, left = pass) on a stacked card made a binary decision physical and fast; the pattern became a generic UI idiom ('Tinder for X') [moderate — widely documented adoption].
- Site: 'It starts with a swipe.™' in Society 115px/700 at -3px tracking, Tinder red on warm grey rgb(240,238,238) — a heavy serif replaces the old flame-gradient look; grey 'Get the app' + black 'Create account' pills (52px radius).
- Nav foregrounds 'Safety' and 'Subscriptions' as top-level items; DNA shows prefers-reduced-motion CSS.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: triage of many similar items (photo culling, inbox sorting, flashcards, candidate shortlists).
- Doesn't: consequential or nuanced choices; critics link swipe volume to gamified, compulsive use — pair with daily limits or curated sets (see Hinge entry for the counter-model).

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Give each product a full-width colour band with its name set huge in itself plus one factual line (families, styles): the homepage becomes a scannable wall of specimens.

## Why it works
- The homepage is a stack of 360px full-width bands, each in its own flat colour pair: 'Spectator' in pale blue italic on bright sky blue, 'New Doric' in hairline pink on plum, 'Orlando' in heavy navy on aqua. Name set in its own face at roughly 150px (from screenshot), centred.
- Under each name, one factual caption ('4 Families, 48 Styles', '6 Families, 84 Styles') in Styrene at ~14px: the scale of the family is the only selling copy.
- Chrome is deliberately utilitarian: Styrene UI 20.9px nav items as dark #323232 tabs with 0px radius and a soft 2px offset shadow (rgba(0,0,0,.35) 2px 2px 7px), and a white 'Commercial Type' logo tab; media covers 83% of the page.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Product lines where each item has a distinct personality and a single quantitative fact (sizes, colourways, editions).
- Doesn't: Catalogues with many near-identical items; colour bands lose meaning when there are dozens.

## Style DNA (optional, compact)
type: Styrene UI A/B 20.9px nav, 13–15px captions, specimen names ~150px · color: one flat bg/fg pair per family band, white page · shape: 0px radius tabs, 2px offset drop shadow · motion: 0.15–0.5s transitions, no reduced-motion CSS

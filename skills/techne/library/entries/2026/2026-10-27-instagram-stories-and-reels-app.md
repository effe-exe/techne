## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Package ephemeral content as a tap-through sequence with a segmented progress bar, so users always know how much is left and can skip at will.

## Why it works
- about.instagram.com: one sentence in Instagram Sans 100/400 at -3.5px tracking, line-height 1.05, with a hand-drawn underline under 'love'; a single outline 'TRY IT NOW' pill whose border is the brand's yellow→magenta gradient — gradient used only as a stroke, never as a fill behind text.
- Off-white rgb(248,246,238) section surfaces, black text, grey rgb(106,113,122); 8/16/26px radii; 0.3s cubic-bezier(0.08,0.52,0.52,1) transitions and prefers-reduced-motion CSS present.
- App pattern (documented, not inspected here): Stories' tap-right/left navigation with top segmented progress bar became a cross-industry convention (news, banking, onboarding). Format originated at Snapchat — credit there for originality.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: short sequential content (recaps, onboarding tips, product updates) where users should control pace.
- Doesn't: reference content people need to revisit or scan; long-form reading.

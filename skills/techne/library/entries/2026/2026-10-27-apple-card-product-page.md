## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Sell a financial product the way you sell hardware: one giant two-line promise, one Apply pill, then the real app screen — no rate tables until the visitor scrolls for them.

## Why it works
- Type scale runs from 12 to 140px; the hero promise "The simplicity of Apple. In a credit card." is SF Pro Display ~96px weight 600, -1.44px tracking, line-height 1.04, centred on a soft pastel mesh gradient (lilac→pink→apricot) that echoes the card's iridescence.
- A single black "Apply now" pill (980px radius) in the hero and the same pill pinned in the product sub-nav (Overview · Features · Family · Monthly Installments · How-To Videos) — one action, always reachable.
- Below the fold the hero is an actual Wallet screen on an iPhone, not a lifestyle shot. Body copy uses translucent black (0.72/0.56/0.8 alpha) for hierarchy instead of multiple greys.
- Motion 0.24–0.8s cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.6,1); prefers-reduced-motion rules present. 17,500px page, 397 links.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: any financial or subscription product that must feel simple; product pages with one conversion action.
- Doesn't: products that compete on rates — burying the APR/fees below the fold would be a trust cost there. Check legal disclosure needs per market.

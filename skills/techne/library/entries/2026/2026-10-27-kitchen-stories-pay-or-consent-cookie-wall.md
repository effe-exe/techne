## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Never make 'accept tracking' or 'pay' the only two doors: a first-layer consent choice needs an equally prominent free 'reject' option.

## Why it works
- First visit is blocked by a modal with two equal green buttons: 'Accept all' (advertising and tracking) or 'Ad-free for 3.99 € / month' via contentpass — no free reject button on the first layer.
- Body text discloses 'up to 205 third-party providers'; 'Reject All' is mentioned only as a way to withdraw consent later, and 'Customize your choice' is a small grey footer link.
- The page behind (recipe grid) is blurred and inert until a choice is made, so the wall gates all content.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: as a counter-example for any consent, paywall or account wall design review; EU regulators have questioned consent-or-pay models for validity of consent.
- Doesn't: not a judgment of the native app's design, which won the ADA in 2017 and was not assessed here.

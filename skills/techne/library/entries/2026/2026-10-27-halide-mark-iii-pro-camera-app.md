## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Borrow the physical instrument's language (dials, tick scales, a single signal colour) so pro controls feel familiar and glanceable instead of hidden in menus.

## Why it works
- Near-black field rgb(2,5,7) with a ghosted, out-of-focus pile of grey camera bodies and lenses behind floating iPad and iPhone renders — the brand is the darkroom/camera bag, not a gradient.
- Proprietary HalideRouterNeue in uppercase at 48px with +1.17px tracking ('UNLOCK YOUR IPHONE CAMERA'); 'MARK III' picked out in a single yellow rgb(255,254,79) — the same yellow used for the checkmark confirm button in the app screenshots.
- Visible in-app UI on the iPad: film-look swatch strip (Valencia etc. with one-line descriptions), EXPOSURE and BALANCE panels drawn as tick-mark scales like a lens barrel.
- Copy positions a clear choice: 'Choose between the default iPhone look or beautiful, film-like…' — tells the user what decision the app adds.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: pro tools that want expert density without menu-diving (audio, video, telemetry, trading terminals): scales, ticks and one accent colour for 'active'.
- Doesn't: novice consumer flows where skeuomorphic instrument metaphors are unfamiliar; tiny tick labels can fail contrast and Dynamic Type if copied literally.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Commit to one metaphor end to end: a 1990s desktop OS (windows, dock, menu-bar clock) makes a radio player, community and shop navigable through rules everyone already knows.

## Why it works
- The player is a classic Mac-style window: title bar with close and zoom boxes, a 591x455 'Airfare_Hotline.avi' video pane, 'Poolsuite: ON AIR ●', track 'Birocratic – Look Both Ways', transport buttons and a channel dropdown marked LIVE.
- The menu bar shows today's date with the year swapped to 1997 ('TUE 27 OCT 1997') and a clock icon: the period conceit is a live datum, not decoration.
- A bottom dock with icon tiles (Player, Newsroom, Mixtapes, Members, Events, Instagram, Vacation, Guestbook, Apps) is the whole IA, and 'BECOME A MEMBER / LOG IN' sits top-left.
- Pixel and bitmap faces (Pixolde, ChiKareGo2, Ishmeria) on pink #F6D5D5 and cream #F9F0E9; 2–4px radii; springy cubic-bezier(0.23,0.09,0.08,1.13) easing; no WebGL.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Media players, community hubs and side projects with a strong period identity; the dock-as-IA idea suits small multi-tool apps.
- Doesn't: Products needing modern accessibility of type (10px bitmap labels) or dense content; the JS-only shell has no fallback.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Treat AI output as a document humans review together: the agent's reply sits in a thread next to the diff, with highlighted phrases carrying teammates' inline comments ('Can we link the benchmark…') — collaboration and verification, not just generation.

## Why it works
- Distinct print identity: plexSerif headline at 25.6px/390, monospace italic taglines ('The world's fastest IDE. Open source. AI optional.'), 'Est 2021 / Est 2026' datelines and a paper-textured pinkish ground — a developer brand that looks like a printed catalogue, not a neon terminal.
- Two product cards side by side (Delta — multiplayer agents; Zed — editor) each with a saturated square (red, blue) and solid Download + ghost Learn More buttons with 4px radii and bottom-edge shading.
- Product shot: three panes — pinned threads and projects, agent conversation with collaborator avatars and an inline comment on a highlighted phrase, and a 'Changes since origin/main +6 −4' diff.
- 'AI optional' is positioning by restraint; caveat: WebGL used and no prefers-reduced-motion CSS detected; mono body at 14px is dense.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: AI features in professional tools where trust and auditability matter (code, legal, research, finance); brands needing to differ from the dark-gradient AI look.
- Doesn't: mass-market consumer apps; paper/print textures can lower contrast if overdone.

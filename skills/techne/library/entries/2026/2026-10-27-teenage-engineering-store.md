## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Strip the product card to image, name and price (or 'sold out') and move personality into the navigation — pictogram menu items with tiny sublinks make wayfinding the brand moment.

## Why it works
- Header is a row of bold black pictograms (four-leaf clover, a shopping-bag-like block, an empty square, a magnifier/dial collage) each paired with a lowercase label (products, store, latest, finder) and 13px sublinks (instruments / audio / designs; visit store / cart & checkout / deals). A block of Japanese mission text and the big TO logo close the row.
- One custom family, te-20, mostly at light weights (button text weight 100, labels 300), all lowercase, 19.1/16/13.2px — a quiet, typewriter-like voice next to loud icons.
- Category filter is plain grey text (all products · pocket operators · field system · … · special offers) with a right-aligned "146 products" count; grid cards are light-grey product tiles with name + price ("life is fantastic! $399") or "sold out", and a thin + to add.
- Almost no chrome: no radii, no shadows, 0.25s/0.15s transitions. Caveat: no reduced-motion rules; 27,000px page, 355 links.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: hardware or object brands with distinctive product photography; small, collectable catalogues.
- Doesn't: pictogram navigation needs labels (as here) — icons alone would fail; weight-100 text is too light for long reading or low-contrast screens.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Prove range with a dense mosaic of real outputs (hexagon tiles of actual charts) instead of describing capabilities, and keep version + GitHub stars visible in the header.

## Why it works
- The bottom half of the fold is a honeycomb of ~25 hexagonal tiles, each a real D3 output (circle packing, Voronoi, streamgraph, contour map, chord diagram, stippled portrait): 'bespoke' is proven, not claimed.
- Headline 'The JavaScript library for bespoke data visualization' in system UI semibold with a hand-drawn orange squiggle under 'bespoke' — one human mark in an otherwise VitePress-standard page.
- Header carries facts: search with a 'Ctrl K' hint, version '7.9.0', 'GitHub 113.8k ★', and a dark 'Made by Observable' pill; CTAs are an orange #EF7234 'Get started' pill plus grey 'What is D3?' and 'Examples' pills (20px radius).
- Neutral system palette (#3C3C43 text at 78% for secondary) lets the colourful examples carry the page; 0.25s ease-out transitions.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Libraries, APIs, design systems, generative tools: any product whose outputs are more persuasive than its feature list.
- Doesn't: Products with a single canonical output; a mosaic of near-identical tiles proves nothing.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Cancellation must be as easy as sign-up, and declining an upsell needs a clear, equal 'No' button — the costliest dark-pattern settlement on record is the proof.

## Why it works
- FTC alleged that checkout interfaces enrolled people in Prime without clear consent and that the multi-step cancellation flow — internally named 'Iliad' after Homer's long war epic — was designed to discourage cancelling [strong — regulator case; Amazon admitted no wrongdoing].
- Settlement (FTC, 25 Sep 2025): $1B civil penalty + up to $1.5B redress; order requires express consent before charging, a clear and conspicuous button to decline Prime, and cancellation as easy as sign-up, with an independent supervisor.
- Follow-up (FTC, 17 Sep 2026): court approved raising the per-customer redress cap from $51 to $200, paid automatically; over $845M paid so far [strong].

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Lesson applies: every subscription upsell at checkout and every cancel flow — one-click decline of equal visual weight, no multi-page retention maze, no ambiguous 'Continue' that means 'subscribe'.
- Retention offers are fine when they're a single, skippable step.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Personality as positioning: a whole site built as a desktop OS (icons, windows, trash) plus candid copy ('97% of users pay us $0') makes a crowded category memorable.

## Why it works
- The site is a desktop: left/right columns of app icons (Home, Pricing, Docs, Demo, Talk to a human / About us, Changelog, Store, Careers, Trash) on a grass wallpaper; content opens in a draggable window with minimize/close controls.
- Pricing honesty at the hero: a 'Set up PostHog for free' card lists '97% of users pay us $0', 'No credit card required', with chunky 3D orange 'Get started' and 'Install with AI' buttons.
- Tabbed demo 'Ask PostHog anything / Give agents product context / Ship with PostHog' shows an AI query box ('Why are signups growing') with a pause control.
- Custom RoundHog family at 36/800 (-0.9px) for H1, 13–17 UI; beige rgb(238,239,233) base, highlighter-yellow text marks behind key phrases; a 'Legally-required cookie banner' states only one first-party cookie is used.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: crowded B2B categories where brand recall matters; companies with a strong, consistent voice.
- Doesn't: conservative buyers or task-focused flows; the OS metaphor adds navigation cost for first-time visitors.

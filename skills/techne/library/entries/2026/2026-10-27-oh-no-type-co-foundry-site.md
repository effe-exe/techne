## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Strip the UI to black and white at two sizes so the fonts' own posters supply all the colour and personality, and let a warm first-person voice do the selling.

## Why it works
- Pure black and white: DNA records only 16px (37 nodes) and 12px (7 nodes), Covik Sans for text and Vulf Mono italic for the lowercase primary nav ('fonts, custom, drawer, sign in'), with uppercase 12px secondary links (INFO, DEMO FONTS, COLLECTIONS).
- The release carousel pairs a short human blurb ('A new geometric grotesque with subtle humanist flavor from our friend Julien Priez. Seven weights, with italics, and a special surprise.') with a dense poster specimen; 'découvrir →' is the only CTA.
- Below, fonts appear as specimen posters or names set in themselves (CAMELION, Bertrand Russell) with uppercase 12px captions on 2px rules; 'drawer' is a playful name for the cart.
- No radii, no shadows; 0.25s linear transitions on 156 nodes; only 6% media coverage, so the type itself is the imagery.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Indie brands with a strong authorial voice; catalogues where the product can be shown as its own graphic.
- Doesn't: Products needing colour-coded status or dense data; the two-size system is too flat for complex UI.

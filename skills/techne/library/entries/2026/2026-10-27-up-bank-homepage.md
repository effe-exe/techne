## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- A bank can have a character: one confident mascot, one colour field and one sentence of values ('built to give, not take') signal a different relationship with money than any feature grid.

## Why it works
- Whole first screen is a coral field rgb(255,112,92) with a yellow rgb(255,238,82) multi-armed triangle mascot in sunglasses (peace signs, OK hands, meditating pose) drawn with thick black outlines — the brand is a personality, not a product shot.
- Headline "Life's Better On The Upside" in Circular 72px weight 900 (lh 1.0, +0.64px tracking) in yellow; one compact paragraph states values and scale ("a bank built to give, not take… for over 1 million Aussies").
- All navigation is collapsed into a single black "MENU" pill (100px radius, Apercu Mono 14px bold caps, 1.68px tracking) with a soft glow, pinned bottom-centre — app-like, thumb-friendly.
- Motion 0.2–0.3s cubic-bezier(0.215,0.61,0.355,1); prefers-reduced-motion rules present; WebGL detected.
- Why technique, not exemplar: hiding every destination behind one MENU pill and leading with a mascot costs findability for anyone arriving with a task.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: challenger brands in conservative categories that need to be remembered; youth-oriented financial products.
- Doesn't: mascots wear thin in high-stakes moments (fraud, hardship); a single-menu landing is wrong for task-driven visitors.

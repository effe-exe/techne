## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Let the headline be a paragraph that switches voice mid-sentence — a bold sans claim followed by a serif explanation — so one typographic gesture carries both the promise and the reasoning.

## Why it works
- Hero is a three-line statement at ~41-52px: 'AI research and products that put safety at the frontier.' in anthropicSans 600, continuing in anthropicSerif 400 ('AI will have a vast impact…') — no CTA competes with the mission.
- Illustration is a halftone compass rose split into ink-grey and mustard halves on a dot-grid card — historical-print imagery as a metaphor for direction and judgement rather than robots or glow.
- Navigation is a row of pale pill buttons (rgb(245,244,237) on rgb(250,249,245), 999px radius, 13px) split left/right of a centred wordmark; 'Try Claude' sits in the nav as a menu, keeping the corporate site distinct from the product.
- Shares the Claude palette (paper rgb(250,249,245), ink rgb(20,20,19), greys rgb(94,93,89)/rgb(135,134,127)); 0.15-0.25s ease-out transitions, WebGL with prefers-reduced-motion CSS.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: mission-led organisations, research labs, policy-heavy brands that must explain before they sell.
- Doesn't: conversion-driven product pages — a paragraph headline with no CTA is wrong when the job is sign-up.

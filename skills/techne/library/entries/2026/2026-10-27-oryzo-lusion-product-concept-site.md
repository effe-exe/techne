## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Satire as a showcase: give a trivial object (a cork coaster) a flagship-launch treatment, so the audience notices the craft because the product is absurd.

## Why it works
- A photoreal WebGL cork coaster sits on a green cutting mat with rulers, pencils, a utility knife and a paperclip; 'ORYZO' in cream Halyard Display at about 150px across the top with 'MADE FOR MUGS. BUILT FOR TABLES.'
- The copy parodies AI launch language: 'The world's most unnecessarily sophisticated cork coaster', 'Powered by AI* … *Adobe Illustrator', 'ORYZO-1: An open-weight model designed to be lightweight and easy to carry'.
- Clear product-page scaffolding despite the joke: INTRO / FEATURES / PRODUCT / CONTACT nav with a dotted active underline, a vertical 'ORYZO-1 MODEL' tab, a 'PLAY' video thumbnail, and 'SCROLL TO CONTINUE'.
- Cream rgb(255,237,215) on espresso rgb(16,9,4) with orange rgb(220,80,0); 36px pill CTAs; 56,691px page. The viewport sets user-scalable=no and there is no reduced-motion CSS.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Studio self-promotion, April-fools or campaign microsites; the product-page scaffolding is a clean template for any launch.
- Doesn't: Real products where the audience needs specs and honesty; do not copy the zoom lock.

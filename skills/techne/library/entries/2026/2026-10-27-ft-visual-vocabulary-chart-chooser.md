## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Organise choices by the user's intent (deviation, correlation, change over time, ranking, distribution, part-to-whole, magnitude, spatial, flow), not by the tool's catalogue of types.

## Why it works
- The page is a single question ('how do we know which one to pick?') followed by nine relationship categories as coloured tabs with matching underline bars: Deviation (yellow), Correlation (tan), Change v Time (green), Ranking (teal), Distribution (orange), Part to whole (blue), Magnitude (purple), Spatial (grey), Flow (olive).
- The intro is honest about scope: 'This list is not meant to be exhaustive, nor a wizard, but a useful starting point' — and credits its source ('Inspired by the Graphic Continuum by Jon Schwabish and Severino Ribecca').
- Typography is spartan (MetricWeb at 60/200 and 36/100 weights, 22px body at a 91ch measure); the colour key is the interface. Charts only appear after a category click, so the first screen is a decision, not a gallery.
- Weakness: the web version is plain, the measure is too long and tab labels collide ('CorrelationChange v Time') at 1440px; the PDF poster remains the better-crafted artifact.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Any chooser or picker (chart types, plans, templates, components) where users know their goal but not the vocabulary.
- Doesn't: Expert tools where users already know the exact item and want direct search.

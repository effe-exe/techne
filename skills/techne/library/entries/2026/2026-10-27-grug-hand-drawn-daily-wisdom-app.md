## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Commit to one handmade material everywhere: grug renders all text, icons and even the status bar from its own hand-drawn stroke data, so a tiny app feels like a world rather than 'a quote app with a cute font'.

## Why it works
- Apple's citation: daily wisdom 'in Neolithic grunts' with a 'scribbled design… a little masterpiece of clever simplicity'; it singles out the hand-drawn status bar and release notes, and notes 'no login, no cloud syncing, nothing extraneous'.
- Per the ADA Q&A, the team found existing handwritten fonts 'too polished', drew their own letters in an in-app font canvas, and built a stroke-data rendering engine used for text, layout, icons and interaction states; lessons animate on like 'ink appearing on paper'.
- Three themes (white, black, mint green); widgets in yellow and teal with doodled sun and sprout; tab bar labelled with dates.
- App Store listing (via search summary): free, no data collected; one wisdom line each sunrise, plus a drawing space and share/widget options.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: small single-purpose apps and brand experiences where character is the product; onboarding or empty states that need warmth.
- Doesn't: content-heavy or data-dense UIs — handwritten rendering hurts legibility, localisation and Dynamic Type if applied wholesale.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- When pricing is usage-based, make the headline number the whole plan: one big rate in a card beside a dark 'Custom' card that lists what is negotiable, instead of a three-tier grid.

## Why it works
- Two cards only: a white "Standard" card whose right half is a single figure ("2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction for domestic cards", ~26px medium) and a navy rgb(10,37,64)-family "Custom" card whose right half is four stacked tiles naming the levers (IC+ pricing, volume, multi-product, country-specific). The choice is self-serve vs. talk-to-us, not Good/Better/Best.
- "No setup fees, monthly fees, or hidden fees" sits directly under the plan name — the trust copy is adjacent to the number it qualifies.
- A floating pill sub-nav (Standard pricing · Custom pricing · FAQs) anchors a 22,500px page with 523 links, so the depth of the fee schedule never feels like a wall.
- Diagonal gradient band runs behind the cards; background is a cool off-white rgb(246,249,252), primary CTA indigo pill, secondary cyan pill on the dark card. DNA detected a prefers-reduced-motion rule on this page.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: transactional/usage pricing (payments, APIs, infrastructure, marketplaces) where tiers would be fake.
- Doesn't: seat-based SaaS where feature differentiation genuinely drives the decision; there a comparison table is clearer.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Sell a durable system by its lifecycle, not its launch: 'Start small. Add to it. Reconfigure it. Take it with you when you move…' — copy that describes the next ten years of ownership.

## Why it works
- Three products, three sections: "606 Universal Shelving System", "620 Chair Programme", "621 Table", each a 32px bold Linotype Univers heading, two short paragraphs and a blue "Learn more →" link. The navigation is literally the catalogue.
- Copy does the selling with provenance and longevity ("designed by Dieter Rams in 1960 and made by Vitsœ ever since"; "Start with an armchair. Add another to make a sofa.").
- Swiss restraint: Univers only (17px body at 1.41 line-height, ~40ch measure), black on white, one link blue rgb(0,115,177), 2px radii, no shadows; real-home photography with books and clutter rather than a staged showroom.
- Caveat: first-layer cookie banner offers Accept and Preferences but no direct Reject; no reduced-motion rules (little motion exists anyway).

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: premium, durable or modular products; any brand whose value is longevity or repairability.
- Doesn't: fast-fashion or promo-driven catalogues; sparse pages need exceptional photography to carry them.

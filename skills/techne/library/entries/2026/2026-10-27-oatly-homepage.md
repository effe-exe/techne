## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Keep the brand voice even in the boring parts: a cookie dialog titled 'We know you didn't come here for cookies' with Decline all and Accept all as twin black buttons.

## Why it works
- The whole page is framed by a diagonal-hatch texture with 1px black box borders and square icon buttons, like a printed form — a texture no competitor uses.
- Hero type is giant condensed caps filled with the same hatch ('THE FUTURE OF TASTE REPORT').
- Consent copy is plain and wry, explains each category in one sentence, and offers 'DECLINE ALL' / 'ACCEPT ALL' as identical black buttons with all optional toggles off; a region popover offers 'Take me to United States' / 'No thanks, stay here'. prefers-reduced-motion CSS present.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Challenger brands whose voice is the differentiator; any consent or error copy.
- Doesn't: Brands needing quiet authority (banks, health); hatch texture lowers readability if used behind body text.

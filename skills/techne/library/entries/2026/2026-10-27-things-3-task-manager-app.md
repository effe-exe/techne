## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Ship an opinionated, small mental model (Today / Upcoming / Anytime / Someday) instead of infinite configurability, and spend the saved complexity on polish.

## Why it works
- Product site mirrors the app's restraint: centered app icon, 'Things' wordmark, one sentence of copy and a single 'Watch Introduction Video' link on a pale blue-grey rgb(242,245,247) field — no pricing table, no feature grid above the fold.
- System font stack (ui-sans-serif = SF on Apple devices) throughout, so the site renders in the same type as the native app; text in muted navy-greys rgba(0,15,36,0.46) and rgb(48,51,54).
- Two Apple Design Awards (2009, 2017) for the same product line are rare durable recognition [moderate as craft signal, weak as performance evidence].
- Site is light on motion (0.1s ease-out transitions only) and has reduced-motion CSS; the app's signature interactions (Magic Plus drag-to-insert) are shown in the video rather than auto-playing.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Personal productivity, habit and planning tools; any product tempted to add settings instead of making decisions.
- Doesn't: Team/workflow tools where users genuinely need custom states and fields; Apple-platform-only, so native feel does not transfer to cross-platform web apps as-is.

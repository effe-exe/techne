## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Encode critical state redundantly in sound, haptics and visuals so a safety signal is never carried by a single sense.

## Why it works
- Apple's citation: 'oko alerts pedestrians to the state of lighted signals through haptic and audio feedback… for those with low to no vision… flawless VoiceOver and Dynamic Type implementation'.
- Third-party descriptions (via search summary): uses the rear camera and on-device AI to read the walk signal; 'differentiable audio, haptic and visual feedback' for each state; works without Wi-Fi or cellular and needs no street infrastructure; can be opened via Siri.
- Developer AYES (Belgium); recognised by Good Design and the EU Road Safety Charter (per search results) [moderate].

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: alerts, status and safety UIs (medical devices, vehicles, payments confirmation): multi-modal, distinct per state, offline-first.
- Doesn't: —; but haptic/audio vocabularies must be learnable and few — too many patterns become noise.

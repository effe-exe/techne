## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Hide heavy computation behind a few switchable lenses on the same data — orbital, map, camera/AR and calendar views of the moon — so each question ('when', 'where', 'what will it look like') has its own simple view.

## Why it works
- Apple's citation praises 'simple elegance', 'best-in-class Liquid Glass integration, and easy onboarding' across iPhone, iPad, Mac, Vision Pro and Watch.
- ADA Q&A screens: an abstract orbital view on deep purple, an Apple Maps overhead view plotting the moon's path over a city, a camera view of moonrise, phase labels like 'Full Supermoon 99.5%', and a month calendar with a phase icon per day.
- Stated intent: 'hide the complexity of astronomical calculations, lunar cycles, and 3D representations behind a simple experience'; team says that with Liquid Glass 'the interface was breathing'.
- Responsive roadmap: after a review said it missed a lunar eclipse, the team added eclipse simulations, visibility maps and guidance (per Q&A).

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: any dashboard over complex models (weather, finance, logistics, astronomy): offer task-shaped views of one dataset instead of one dense screen.
- Doesn't: if views diverge in data or units, multiple lenses confuse; keep labels and numbers consistent across views.

## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Make the navigation itself a brand artefact: a few large line-drawn pictograms, each with a lowercase label and three plain sublinks, so the menu doubles as the identity.

## Why it works
- Top band on pure black: four sections ('products', 'store', 'latest', 'finder'), each a thin line pictogram (flower, box, square, magnifier/arrows) with a ~40px lowercase label and three 16px sublinks ('instruments / audio / designs').
- A small Japanese mission paragraph and the outline logo sit in the same row; below, one spotlit product photograph on black fills the page (media coverage 1.01).
- DNA: only custom faces te-20 and TechnoType; three sizes (16 / 19.1 / 39.7px); single 7.3px radius; prefers-reduced-motion CSS present; transitions 0.15–0.5s.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Brands with a distinctive drawing or type language and a small, stable set of top-level sections.
- Doesn't: Large catalogues or SEO-driven commerce; pictogram nav does not scale past ~6 items.

type: te-20 / TechnoType, 3 sizes 16/19/40, all lowercase · color: #000 with off-white rgb(241,242,242) · shape: hairline pictograms, ~0 radius · motion: minimal, reduced-motion respected

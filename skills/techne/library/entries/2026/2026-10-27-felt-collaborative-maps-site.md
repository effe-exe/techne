## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Turn the domain's own instrument readout into chrome: a thin top bar with live LAT/LON/BRG/ZOOM/CRS and a scale bar tells map people 'this is for you' before any copy.

## Why it works
- A 28px black strip above the nav reads like a GIS status bar in SF Mono: 'LAT 37.8182° N · LON 122.2866° W · BRG 045.8° · ZOOM 11.0' on the left and 'CRS EPSG:4326' with a '3 km' scale bar on the right — the brand's credibility signal is a piece of the product.
- Headline 'The spatial infrastructure your business runs on' in GT Alpina serif at 69px, tracking -1.38px, on warm paper #F4F2EA with #1C1B18 text; an 'AI-NATIVE GIS' eyebrow in mono rust #A7531B.
- The hero image is a soft, illustrated aerial map (river, street grid, trees) fading into the page, with 7-step pagination dots; CTA pair is solid black 'Get started for free' and outlined 'Book a demo →' at 4px radius.
- Four-family system with clear roles: Atlas Grotesk UI (171 nodes), SF Mono data (75), GT Alpina display (21), Publico Text long-form (5); micro-transitions at 0.14s and a springy cubic-bezier(0.2,0.9,0.3,1.3) on 23 nodes.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Vertical/pro tools (mapping, audio, finance, lab software) that can borrow their users' instrument language as brand.
- Doesn't: Consumer products whose users do not recognise the instrument vocabulary; it would read as noise.

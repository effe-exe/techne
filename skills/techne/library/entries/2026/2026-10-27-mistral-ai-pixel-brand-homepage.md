## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Build a whole brand world from one constraint: a visible layout grid filled with stepped orange-to-yellow blocks and tiny pixel sprites (cat, castle, flag) — instantly recognisable without photography or 3D.

## Why it works
- The page grid is drawn: hairline column and row rules frame nav items as cells, a corner arc and registration dots mark the hero box, and the logo strip below sits in bordered cells.
- Colour is a strict warm ramp — rgb(255,82,41) red-orange, rgb(255,130,4) orange, rgb(255,175,1) amber — stacked as stepped blocks and rotated diamonds against a warm white rgb(251,251,248).
- Pixel sprites (a black cat, a pixel flag inline in the headline 'Mistral [flag] is #1 in Sovereign AI.', a castle, a factory) give personality; ALTMistral display 56px with Inter UI.
- Weakness: superlative positioning headline and no product (Le Chat) visible on the fold; 0.15-0.5s standard ease, reduced-motion CSS present.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: brands needing a distinctive, ownable system on a small budget; playful developer or infrastructure brands.
- Doesn't: products whose hero must demonstrate the UI; pixel art reads as retro/gaming in some contexts.

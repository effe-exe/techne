## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Let one typeface carry all chrome at two sizes and hand the whole fold to a single art-directed image labelled with the font it sells.

## Why it works
- The fold is one full-bleed art-directed image (blurred black ladders on a vermilion #D33C03-range ground) with a tiny 'Die Grotesk' label bottom-left: the typeface is sold by atmosphere, not by a pangram.
- Extreme type economy: the DNA records only two sizes on the page, 16px (117 nodes) and 36px (2), all in Söhne (the foundry's own face) on black, with greys #8D8D8D/#7F7F7F for secondary text.
- Chrome is reduced to 'Klim Type Foundry · Fonts' and a hamburger in a 48px black bar; the only radius in use is 2px (46 nodes); no shadows, no transitions.
- Weakness: no headline or value proposition at the fold, and no reduced-motion CSS detected; a first-time visitor must already know what Klim is.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Brands with a strong reputation selling an intangible craft (type, sound, scent) where restraint signals confidence.
- Doesn't: Unknown products that need to explain themselves; a label-only fold would leave visitors guessing.

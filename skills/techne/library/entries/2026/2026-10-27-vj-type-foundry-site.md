## What to steal (principle, 1–3 bullets)
- Lead with the type in use (newspapers, posters, books in real photographs) rather than pangrams: real context sells a display face faster than a specimen.

## Why it works
- The homepage is a three-column grid of in-use images on a warm off-white #ECEBE4: a model reading a fashion newspaper set in the foundry's serif, a 'KYOTO / MAKO' poster on brown, a yellow 'ACTI-VATING ARCHIVES' editorial spread.
- Navigation is one face ('Category', 147 nodes) at 12px uppercase with 2.88px tracking (TYPEFACES with a superscript '14' count, ABOUT, LICENSES, IN-USE), over a 1px rule.
- Colour comes only from the imagery (DNA surfaces: olive, forest #2C6D51, rust #943E05, pink, yellow); the chrome itself stays black on cream.
- Clarity cost: no headline or explicit 'shop' prompt at the fold; first-time visitors must infer that these images are font specimens.

## Where it applies / where it doesn't
- Applies: Brands whose product is best judged in context (type, textiles, paint, furniture).
- Doesn't: Products where users need specs before mood.
